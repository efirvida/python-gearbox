# ISO 6336-3: tooth bending strength

{ref}`ISO 6336-3:2019 <ref-iso6336-3>` answers one question: *will the tooth break at the root?*
It computes the tensile stress in the root fillet of each gear, the stress the material can stand,
and the ratio of the two. This page follows the standard clause by clause, with the formula numbers
of the 2019 edition, and says for each step what python-gearbox does. The influence factors that
come from part 1 ($K_A$, $K_\gamma$, $K_v$, $K_{F\beta}$, $K_{F\alpha}$) are explained in
{doc}`iso6336-1`; the surface counterpart of this page is {doc}`iso6336-2`.

Only **method B** is implemented. Method A (finite elements, strain gauges, tests of the real gear)
is outside a closed-form calculation; the library's own finite-element model is described in
{doc}`../fem/index`.

## The idea

A tooth loaded at its flank behaves like a short, stubby cantilever beam clamped in the rim. The
bending moment is largest where the tooth meets the rim, and the stress is largest on the tensile
side of the root fillet. Method B reduces this to four decisions:

1. **Where the load acts.** At the *outer point of single pair tooth contact* (the point where the
   mating tooth pair last carries the load alone), because it has the longest lever arm while one
   pair still takes the whole force. For helical gears the same is done on the *virtual spur gear*
   of the normal section, with the normal load spread over the face width.
2. **Which section is critical.** The section whose chord joins the two points where tangents at
   30° to the tooth axis touch the root fillets (60° for internal gears). Its chord $s_{Fn}$, the
   fillet radius $\rho_F$ there and the lever arm $h_{Fe}$ of the load are the whole geometry.
3. **A Lewis-like nominal stress.** The bending stress of that beam, $6 F h_{Fe}\cos\alpha_{Fen} /
   (b\,s_{Fn}^2)$, written as $F_t/(b\,m_n)$ times a dimensionless form factor $Y_F$.
4. **A stress concentration on top.** The fillet raises the local stress above the beam value by
   the stress correction factor $Y_S$, which depends on how sharp the fillet is ($q_s =
   s_{Fn}/2\rho_F$) and on how slender the tooth is ($L = s_{Fn}/h_{Fe}$).

## Tooth root stress

The stress at the root is the nominal stress times the load factors, formula (3), and the nominal
stress is formula (4):

$$
\sigma_F = \sigma_{F0}\, K_A K_\gamma K_v K_{F\beta} K_{F\alpha} \tag{3}
$$

$$
\sigma_{F0} = \frac{F_t}{b\, m_n}\, Y_F\, Y_S\, Y_\beta\, Y_B\, Y_{DT} \tag{4}
$$

$\sigma_{F0}$ is the nominal tooth root stress, the maximum local principal stress when an error-free
pair carries the static nominal torque (stress ratio $R = 0$). It is computed separately for the
pinion and the wheel: both carry the same $F_t$, but each has its own $Y_F$, $Y_S$ and $Y_B$ and
possibly its own $b$.

| Symbol | Meaning | Where it comes from |
|:--|:--|:--|
| $F_t$ | nominal tangential force at the reference cylinder | torque and reference diameter |
| $b$ | load-bearing face width, $b = 2 b_B$ for double helical gears | see below |
| $m_n$ | normal module | gear |
| $Y_F$ | form factor | formula (9) |
| $Y_S$ | stress correction factor | formula (62) |
| $Y_\beta$ | helix angle factor | formula (66) |
| $Y_B$ | rim thickness factor | formulae (68)-(71) |
| $Y_{DT}$ | deep tooth factor | formulae (72)-(74) |
| $K_A K_\gamma K_v K_{F\beta} K_{F\alpha}$ | application, mesh load, dynamic, face load and transverse load factors | ISO 6336-1, {doc}`iso6336-1` |

**Face width.** $b$ is the width at the root circle, ignoring chamfers and end rounding. When the
two gears are not equally wide, the load-bearing width of the wider one is taken as the width of the
narrower one plus at most one module at each end (note to formula (4)).

### In python-gearbox

{py:func}`~gearbox.standards.iso6336.bending.calculate_bending` evaluates (3) and (4) for both
gears. $F_t$ is {py:attr}`~gearbox.transmission.Transmission.nominal_tangential_force` and
$K_A K_\gamma$ is {py:attr}`~gearbox.transmission.Transmission.external_load_factor`
(`application_factor` times `mesh_load_factor`); $K_v$, $K_{F\beta}$ and $K_{F\alpha}$ come from the
ISO 6336-1 load factors of the same transmission. The face width $b$ is the narrower width plus one
module per end, limited to the gear's own width and doubled for double helical gears (`face_width`
is then the width $b_B$ of *one* helix). The result gives the factors one by one, so
$\sigma_F$ can be rebuilt by hand; with the numbers of the example below,
$F_t/(b\,m_n) = 9579 / (34 \cdot 2.5) = 112.7$ MPa, times $Y_F Y_S = 1.530 \cdot 1.848$, times
$K_A K_v K_{F\beta} = 1.3 \cdot 1.028 \cdot 1.072$, gives the 457 MPa of the pinion.

## Geometry of the critical section

### Virtual gear and load point

Helical gears are treated as spur gears in the normal section. The virtual gear has the base helix
angle, number of teeth and contact ratio

$$
\beta_b = \arcsin(\sin\beta\,\cos\alpha_n) \tag{15}
$$

$$
z_n = \frac{z}{\cos^2\beta_b\,\cos\beta} \tag{16}
$$

$$
\varepsilon_{\alpha n} = \frac{\varepsilon_\alpha}{\cos^2\beta_b} \tag{17}
$$

and the diameters of the virtual gear follow from them:

$$
\begin{aligned}
d_n &= \frac{d}{\cos^2\beta_b} = m_n z_n \tag{18}\\
p_{bn} &= \pi\, m_n \cos\alpha_n \tag{19}\\
d_{bn} &= d_n \cos\alpha_n \tag{20}\\
d_{an} &= d_n + d_{Na} - d \tag{21}
\end{aligned}
$$

The load point is the outer point of single pair contact. On the line of action it lies one
base pitch less the part of the contact ratio above one, $p_{bn}(\varepsilon_{\alpha n} - 1)$, below
the tip, which gives its diameter $d_{en}$, pressure angle $\alpha_{en}$, the half tooth angle
$\gamma_e$ there and the load angle $\alpha_{Fen}$ (the angle between the load direction and the
tangential direction at the tooth axis, figure above):

$$
d_{en} = 2\,\frac{z}{|z|}\sqrt{\left[\sqrt{\left(\tfrac{d_{an}}{2}\right)^2 - \left(\tfrac{d_{bn}}{2}\right)^2}
- \frac{\pi\, d \cos\beta \cos\alpha_n}{|z|}\,(\varepsilon_{\alpha n} - 1)\right]^2 + \left(\tfrac{d_{bn}}{2}\right)^2} \tag{22}
$$

$$
\alpha_{en} = \arccos\frac{d_{bn}}{d_{en}} \tag{23}
$$

$$
\gamma_e = \frac{0.5\pi + 2 x \tan\alpha_n}{z_n} + \operatorname{inv}\alpha_n - \operatorname{inv}\alpha_{en} \tag{24}
$$

$$
\alpha_{Fen} = \alpha_{en} - \gamma_e \tag{25}
$$

The number of teeth $z$ is positive for external and negative for internal gears, which makes
$d_{en}$ negative for internal gears. If the tooth tip is rounded or chamfered, $d_a$ in (21) is
replaced by the effective tip diameter $d_{Na}$.

### Gear cut with a hob (or rack cutter)

For an external gear generated by a rack the critical section follows from the rack's dimensions:
its dedendum $h_{fP}$ (that is, the part of the rack that cuts the gear root), its tip radius
$\rho_{fP}$, and the residual fillet undercut $s_{pr}$ (zero unless the gear is cut by a
protuberance tool). Two auxiliary lengths and an auxiliary quantity $G$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
E &= \frac{\pi}{4}\, m_n - h_{fP}\tan\alpha_n + \frac{s_{pr}}{\cos\alpha_n} - (1 - \sin\alpha_n)\,\frac{\rho_{fP}}{\cos\alpha_n} \tag{26}\\[4pt]
G &= \frac{\rho_{fP}}{m_n} - \frac{h_{fP}}{m_n} + x \tag{27}\\[4pt]
H &= \frac{2}{z_n}\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - \frac{E}{m_n}\right) - T, \qquad T = \frac{\pi}{3}\ \text{(external gears)} \tag{28}
\end{aligned}
$$

The angle $\theta$ of the tangent point on the generating trochoid is the solution of

$$
\theta = \frac{2G}{z_n}\tan\theta - H \tag{29}
$$

which the standard solves by fixed-point iteration from $\theta = \pi/6$ (about five iterations). Then

$$
\frac{s_{Fn}}{m_n} = z_n \sin\!\left(\frac{\pi}{3} - \theta\right) + \sqrt{3}\left(\frac{G}{\cos\theta} - \frac{\rho_{fP}}{m_n}\right) \tag{30}
$$

$$
\frac{\rho_F}{m_n} = \frac{\rho_{fP}}{m_n} + \frac{2G^2}{\cos\theta\,(z_n\cos^2\theta - 2G)} \tag{31}
$$

$$
\frac{h_{Fe}}{m_n} = \frac12\left[(\cos\gamma_e - \sin\gamma_e\tan\alpha_{Fen})\frac{d_{en}}{m_n} - z_n\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi}{3} - \theta\right) - \left(\frac{G}{\cos\theta} - \frac{\rho_{fP}}{m_n}\right)\right] \tag{32}
$$

$s_{Fn}$ is the chord between the two 30° tangent points, $\rho_F$ the fillet radius there and
$h_{Fe}$ the distance from the chord to the point where the load line meets the tooth axis.

### Gear cut with a shaper cutter

A pinion-type shaper cutter does not produce a straight-flanked rack profile, so clause 6.2.4
constructs the generation explicitly: the gear and the cutter roll in a *manufacturing mesh*, the
rounded tip corner $M$ of the cutter cuts the fillet, and the tangent point is found on the
envelope. Internal gears are always shaper-cut (clause 6.1 e), and clause 6.2.5 says to use the
same formulae with the negative signs of internal gearing and $\theta = 60°$.

:::{dropdown} Formulae (33) to (61)

Cutter data: number of teeth $z_0$, profile shift $x_0$, tip corner radius $\rho_{a0}$, tip height
$h_{aP0}$ and residual undercut $s_{pr}$. Tangential angle $\theta$: 30° for external, 60° for
internal gears.

Manufacturing mesh of cutter and gear:

$$
\begin{aligned}
z_{0v} &= \frac{z_0}{\cos\beta\,\cos^2\beta_b} \tag{33}\\
\xi &= 2\,\frac{x_0 + x}{z_{0v} + z_n}\tan\alpha_n + \operatorname{inv}\alpha_n \tag{34}\\
\alpha_{w0} &= \sqrt[3]{3\xi}\ \text{(start)}, \qquad
\alpha_{w0} := \alpha_{w0} + \frac{\xi - \operatorname{inv}\alpha_{w0}}{\tan^2\alpha_{w0}} \tag{35, 36}\\
a_0 &= m_n\,\frac{z_{0v} + z_n}{2}\,\frac{\cos\alpha_n}{\cos\alpha_{w0}} \tag{37}\\
u_0 &= \frac{z_{0v}}{z_n} \tag{38}\\
r_w &= \frac{a_0}{1 + u_0}, \qquad r_{w0} = r_w u_0 \tag{39}\\
r_{b0} &= 0.5\, m_n z_{0v}\cos\alpha_n \tag{40}
\end{aligned}
$$

Tip corner $M$ of the cutter (centre of the tip rounding):

$$
\begin{aligned}
r_M &= r_{a0} - \rho_{a0} = m_n\left(\frac{z_{0v}}{2} + \frac{h_{aP0}}{m_n} + x_0 - \frac{\rho_{a0}}{m_n}\right) \tag{41, 42}\\
\alpha_M &= \arccos\frac{r_{b0}}{r_M} \tag{43}\\
\Delta\alpha &= \frac{0.5\pi + 2 x_0\tan\alpha_n}{z_{0v}} - \frac{\rho_{a0} - s_{pr}}{r_{b0}} + \operatorname{inv}\alpha_n - \operatorname{inv}\alpha_M \tag{44}\\
X_M &= r_M\sin\Delta\alpha, \qquad Y_M = r_M\cos\Delta\alpha \tag{45}
\end{aligned}
$$

Auxiliary angle $\psi$ by Newton iteration (two steps are generally enough; stop at
$|y/y'| < 10^{-6}$):

$$
\begin{aligned}
\psi_0 &= \frac{\pi}{z_n} + \theta, \qquad \psi = \psi_0 \tag{46, 47}\\
\lambda &= \frac{r_{w0}}{r_M}\cos\psi \tag{48}\\
y &= \psi - \arccos\lambda + \Delta\alpha + \frac{\psi - \psi_0}{u_0} \tag{49}\\
y' &= 1 + \frac{1}{u_0} - \frac{r_{w0}}{r_M}\,\frac{\sin\psi}{\sqrt{1 - \lambda^2}} \tag{50}\\
\psi &:= \psi - \frac{y}{y'} \tag{51}
\end{aligned}
$$

Distance $K$ from $M$ to the contact point of the pitch circles:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\omega_0 &= \delta - \psi - \Delta\alpha, \qquad \delta = \arccos\!\left(\frac{r_{w0}}{r_M}\cos\psi\right) \tag{52, 53}\\
\Delta h' &= Y_M - r_{w0}\cos\omega_0 \tag{54}\\
\Delta h &= \frac{\Delta h'\sin\psi}{\sin(\psi + \omega_0)} \tag{55}\\
K &= \frac{\Delta h}{\sin\psi} \tag{56}
\end{aligned}
$$

Tangent point, chord, fillet radius and bending arm:

$$
\begin{aligned}
X &= r_w\sin(\psi - \theta) - (K + \rho_{a0})\cos\theta \tag{57}\\
Y &= r_w\cos(\psi - \theta) - (K + \rho_{a0})\sin\theta \tag{58}\\
s_{Fn} &= 2X \tag{59}\\
\rho_F &= \frac{K^2}{\dfrac{r_{w0}\, r_w}{r_{w0} + r_w}\sin\psi + K} + \rho_{a0} \tag{60}\\
h_{Fe} &= (\cos\gamma_e - \sin\gamma_e\tan\alpha_{Fen})\,\frac{d_{en}}{2} - Y \tag{61}
\end{aligned}
$$

:::

### In python-gearbox

`ToothRootGeometry` bundles the result for one gear, and the private helper
`gearbox.standards.iso6336.bending._tooth_root_geometry(transmission, gear)` computes it. It follows
the formulae above and chooses the branch from {py:attr}`~gearbox.models.Tool.cutter_type`:

* **Hob.** Formulae (26)-(32), with the tool data of {py:class}`~gearbox.models.Tool`:
  $h_{fP}$ is `dedendum_coefficient` $\cdot\, m_n$, $\rho_{fP}$ is `root_fillet_radius_coefficient`
  $\cdot\, m_n$ and $s_{pr}$ is `residual_fillet_undercut_coefficient` $\cdot\, m_n$. $x$ is the
  gear's `profile_shift_coefficient`. The iteration (29) runs nine times from $\pi/6$ without a
  convergence test (the standard says about five).
* **Shaper cutter** (`Tool(cutter_type="shaper", cutter_teeth=z_0, ...)`). Formulae (33)-(61):
  $x_0$ is `Tool.profile_shift_coefficient`, $\rho_{a0}$ is `tool_edge_radius_coefficient` $\cdot\,
  m_n$ (by default the root fillet radius) and $h_{aP0}$ is read from `dedendum_coefficient`
  $\cdot\, m_n$ (the tip height of the cutter equals the dedendum of the basic rack of the gear).
  The Newton iteration (46)-(51) stops at $10^{-14}$ instead of $10^{-6}$.
* **Internal gears** (`Gear.internal=True` on the wheel) go through the shaper branch with
  $\theta = 60°$, $z_n < 0$ and the signed diameters. The cutter must have fewer teeth than the
  gear, and a hob raises `ValueError`, as clause 6.1 e) demands.

The load point uses formulae (15)-(25) with the pinion's helix angle for both gears (they are
equal), $\varepsilon_\alpha$ from {py:attr}`~gearbox.transmission.Transmission.transverse_contact_ratio`
and the tip diameter $d_a$ in place of $d_{Na}$ (tip chamfers and rounding are not modelled).

## Form factor $Y_F$

Combining the beam stress at the critical section with the nominal stress of formula (4) gives
formula (9):

$$
Y_F = \frac{\dfrac{6\,h_{Fe}}{m_n}\cos\alpha_{Fen}}{\left(\dfrac{s_{Fn}}{m_n}\right)^{2}\cos\alpha_n}\; f_\varepsilon \tag{9}
$$

The first factor is the Lewis form factor of this tooth: a slender tooth (large $h_{Fe}$, small
$s_{Fn}$) has a large $Y_F$. $f_\varepsilon$ is new in the 2019 edition (the standard gives it no
other name than "the factor $f_\varepsilon$"; it "considers the influence of load distribution
between the teeth in the mesh"). It matters when the virtual contact ratio $\varepsilon_{\alpha n}$
reaches 2 or more, which happens for helical gears with high helix angle or high contact ratio:
the load is then shared by more than two pairs and the root stress drops. It is one for ordinary
spur gears, and formulae (10)-(14) join the cases smoothly:

| Case | $f_\varepsilon$ | Formula |
|:--|:--|:--|
| $\varepsilon_\beta = 0$ and $\varepsilon_{\alpha n} < 2$ | $1$ | (10) |
| $\varepsilon_\beta = 0$ and $\varepsilon_{\alpha n} \ge 2$ | $0.7$ | (11) |
| $0 < \varepsilon_\beta < 1$ and $\varepsilon_{\alpha n} < 2$ | $\left(1 - \varepsilon_\beta + \varepsilon_\beta/\varepsilon_{\alpha n}\right)^{0.5}$ | (12) |
| $0 < \varepsilon_\beta < 1$ and $\varepsilon_{\alpha n} \ge 2$ | $\left(\dfrac{1-\varepsilon_\beta}{2} + \dfrac{\varepsilon_\beta}{\varepsilon_{\alpha n}}\right)^{0.5}$ | (13) |
| $\varepsilon_\beta \ge 1$ | $\varepsilon_{\alpha n}^{-0.5}$ | (14) |

The standard states that determining $Y_F$ by graphical means is not recommended; $\theta$ should be
iterated.

In python-gearbox, $Y_F$ is computed per gear from its `ToothRootGeometry`, $\alpha_n$ from the
transmission and $f_\varepsilon$ from $\varepsilon_\beta$
({py:attr}`~gearbox.transmission.Transmission.overlap_ratio`, with $b_B$ for double helical gears)
and $\varepsilon_{\alpha n}$ of formula (17). The test $\varepsilon_\beta = 0$ is exact, so a helical
gear is never mistaken for a spur gear.

## Stress correction factor $Y_S$

$Y_S$ converts the nominal stress to the local stress in the fillet. It covers the stress amplifying
effect of the change of section at the fillet and the fact that the real stress state in the
critical section is more complex than the simple beam picture. Formula (62), with (63) and (64):

$$
Y_S = (1.2 + 0.13\,L)\; q_s^{\,\dfrac{1}{1.21 + 2.3/L}} \tag{62}
$$

$$
L = \frac{s_{Fn}}{h_{Fe}} \tag{63}
$$

$$
q_s = \frac{s_{Fn}}{2\,\rho_F} \tag{64}
$$

It is valid for $1 \le q_s < 8$, was derived for external spur gears with 20° pressure angle, and
gives approximate values for internal gears and other pressure angles (clause 7.1). Graphical
determination is not appropriate. $s_{Fn}$, $h_{Fe}$ and $\rho_F$ are those of the hob formulae
(30)-(32) or of the shaper formulae (59)-(61).

**Grinding notch.** A grinding notch of depth $t_g$ and radius $\rho_g$ near the critical section
raises the stress further. If it is at or below the point where the 30° tangent touches the
fillet, $Y_S$ is replaced by $Y_{Sg}$, formula (65), valid for $\sqrt{t_g/\rho_g} < 2.0$:

$$
Y_{Sg} = \frac{1.3\, Y_S}{1.3 - 0.6\sqrt{t_g/\rho_g}} \tag{65}
$$

In python-gearbox set `Gear.grinding_notch_depth` ($t_g$) and `grinding_notch_radius` ($\rho_g$) and
$Y_S$ is replaced by $Y_{Sg}$ in the result; a ratio of 2.0 or more raises `ValueError`. The code
cannot tell where the notch sits, so give the notch only when it is near the critical section. The
range $1 \le q_s < 8$ is not checked.

## Helix angle, rim and deep tooth factors

**Helix angle factor $Y_\beta$** (clause 8). The virtual spur gear overestimates the root stress of a
helical gear, whose contact lines are oblique. With the reference helix angle $\beta$ in degrees
and the overlap ratio $\varepsilon_\beta > 0$, formula (66):

$$
Y_\beta = \left(1 - \varepsilon_\beta\,\frac{\beta}{120^\circ}\right)\frac{1}{\cos^3\beta} \tag{66}
$$

The value 1.0 is substituted for $\varepsilon_\beta$ when $\varepsilon_\beta > 1$, and $30°$ for
$\beta$ when $\beta > 30°$. Values for $\beta > 25°$ shall be confirmed by experience (clause 8.2).

**Rim thickness factor $Y_B$** (clause 9) derates thin-rimmed gears, where fatigue can start in
the rim instead of the fillet. $s_R$ is the rim thickness and $h_t$ the tooth height. External gears,
formulae (68) and (69):

$$
Y_B = 1 \ \ \text{for } s_R/h_t \ge 1.2, \qquad Y_B = 1.6\,\ln\!\left(2.242\,\frac{h_t}{s_R}\right) \ \ \text{for } 0.5 < s_R/h_t < 1.2
$$

$s_R/h_t \le 0.5$ shall be avoided. Internal gears, formulae (70) and (71), with the module instead of
the tooth height:

$$
Y_B = 1 \ \ \text{for } s_R/m_n \ge 3.5, \qquad Y_B = 1.15\,\ln\!\left(8.324\,\frac{m_n}{s_R}\right) \ \ \text{for } 1.75 < s_R/m_n < 3.5
$$

with $s_R/m_n \le 1.75$ to be avoided. The scope of the standard is $s_R > 0.5\,h_t$ (external) and
$s_R > 1.75\,m_n$ (internal).

**Deep tooth factor $Y_{DT}$** (clause 10) lowers the stress of high-precision gears with
$2 \le \varepsilon_{\alpha n} < 2.5$ that have an actual profile modification giving a trapezoidal
load along the path of contact. It applies only to ISO tolerance class 4 or better, and if the
classes of the two gears differ the worse one is used for both. Formulae (72) to (74):

$$
Y_{DT} = \begin{cases}
1.0 & \varepsilon_{\alpha n} \le 2.05\ \text{or tolerance class} > 4 \\
-0.666\,\varepsilon_{\alpha n} + 2.366 & 2.05 < \varepsilon_{\alpha n} \le 2.5 \\
0.7 & \varepsilon_{\alpha n} > 2.5
\end{cases}
$$

In python-gearbox $Y_\beta$ uses the pinion's `helix_angle` and $\varepsilon_\beta$ of the pair.
$Y_B$ is 1 for a solid disc (`rim_thickness=None`) and otherwise follows the rim formulae with
`Gear.rim_thickness` and `Gear.tooth_height`; it raises `ValueError` below the limit of 0.5 (external)
or 1.75 (internal; the rim thickness is then mandatory). $Y_{DT}$ uses the worse
`quality_grade` of the two gears; the code does not check whether a profile modification exists, so
set a quality grade above 4 if the gears have none.

## Permissible stress and safety factor

### Safety factor

The safety factor is computed separately for each gear, formulae (1) and (2), and must reach the
minimum $S_{F\min}$ agreed between manufacturer and customer (ISO 6336-1 clause 4.1.11). Since tooth
breakage usually ends the life of a transmission, $S_{F\min}$ should be larger than the minimum
safety factor against pitting (clause 4):

$$
S_F = \frac{\sigma_{FG}}{\sigma_F} \ge S_{F\min}, \qquad \sigma_{FG} = \sigma_{FP}\, S_{F\min}
$$

### Permissible stress, method B

Formula (5) derives the permissible stress from the nominal stress number of the reference test
gears and corrects it for the differences between that gear and the one of interest:

$$
\sigma_{FP} = \frac{\sigma_{F\lim}\, Y_{ST}\, Y_{NT}}{S_{F\min}}\; Y_{\delta\,rel\,T}\, Y_{R\,rel\,T}\, Y_X \tag{5}
$$

| Symbol | Meaning |
|:--|:--|
| $\sigma_{F\lim}$ | nominal stress number (bending) from reference test gears, see {ref}`ISO 6336-5 <ref-iso6336-5>` |
| $Y_{ST}$ | stress correction factor of the reference test gear, $Y_{ST} = 2.0$ (clause 7.4) |
| $Y_{NT}$ | life factor for the reference test gear (clause 12) |
| $Y_{\delta\,rel\,T}$ | relative notch sensitivity factor (clause 13) |
| $Y_{R\,rel\,T}$ | relative surface factor (clause 14) |
| $Y_X$ | size factor (clause 15) |

**Life factor $Y_{NT}$.** It raises the permissible stress when fewer load cycles $N_L$ than
$3 \times 10^6$ are required. Table 3 gives its value for the static load, for $3 \times 10^6$ cycles
(1.0) and for $10^{10}$ cycles (0.85 to 1.0, the lower value for critical service). In between the
standard interpolates on a log-log scale (figure 11); formulae (6) to (8) write the same thing as
$\sigma_{FP} = \sigma_{FP\,ref}\,(3\cdot10^6/N_L)^{\exp}$ with

$$
\exp = 0.4037\,\log\frac{\sigma_{FP\,stat}}{\sigma_{FP\,ref}} \ \ (10^4 < N_L \le 3\cdot10^6;\ \text{St, V, GTS, GGG perl./bai.}), \qquad
\exp = 0.2876\,\log\frac{\sigma_{FP\,stat}}{\sigma_{FP\,ref}} \ \ (10^3 < N_L \le 3\cdot10^6;\ \text{others})
$$

The standard does not apply above stress levels permissible for $10^3$ cycles, where the elastic
limit of the tooth may be exceeded (clause 4).

**Relative notch sensitivity $Y_{\delta\,rel\,T}$** (clause 13). It says how much more or less
sensitive to the sharpness of the fillet the material of the gear is than the reference gear, whose
notch parameter is $q_{sT} = 2.5$ (which corresponds to $Y_S = 2.0$). For the reference stress,
formulae (75) to (77):

$$
Y_{\delta\,rel\,T} = \frac{1 + \sqrt{\rho'\,\chi^*}}{1 + \sqrt{\rho'\,\chi^*_T}}, \qquad
\chi^* = \chi^*_P\,(1 + 2 q_s), \qquad \chi^*_P = \frac{1}{5}\ \text{mm}^{-1} \tag{75, 76, 77}
$$

with $\chi^*_T$ obtained from the same expression with $q_s = q_{sT} = 2.5$. The slip layer thickness
$\rho'$ depends on the material, and on its strength, through table 4 (values between the
rows may be interpolated): for example 0.3124 mm for GG with $\sigma_B = 150$ N/mm², 0.1005 mm for NT
and NV, 0.0445 mm for St with $\sigma_S = 400$ N/mm² and 0.003 mm for Eh and IF.

For the **static** stress, $Y_{\delta\,rel\,T}$ depends on $Y_S$ instead (formulae (78) to (83)):

| Material | $Y_{\delta\,rel\,T}$ | Formula |
|:--|:--|:--|
| St with well-defined yield point $\sigma_S$ | $\dfrac{1 + 0.93\,(Y_S - 1)\sqrt[4]{200/\sigma_S}}{1 + 0.93\sqrt[4]{200/\sigma_S}}$ | (78) |
| St with steadily increasing elongation curve, V, GGG (perl., bai.), with the 0.2 % proof stress $\sigma_{0.2}$ | $\dfrac{1 + 0.82\,(Y_S - 1)\sqrt[4]{300/\sigma_{0.2}}}{1 + 0.82\sqrt[4]{300/\sigma_{0.2}}}$ | (79) |
| Eh, IF (root) | $0.44\,Y_S + 0.12$ | (80) |
| NT, NV | $0.20\,Y_S + 0.60$ | (81) |
| GTS | $0.075\,Y_S + 0.85$ | (82) |
| GG, GGG (ferr.) | $1.0$ | (83) |

Formulae (78) and (79) hold only if the local stresses do not reach the yield point.

**Relative surface factor $Y_{R\,rel\,T}$** (clause 14). The reference gears have a root roughness
$Rz = 10\ \mu$m; a smoother fillet gains strength and a rougher one loses it. For $Rz < 1\ \mu$m it
is constant, formulae (84) to (86), and for $1 \le Rz \le 40\ \mu$m formulae (87) to (89):

| Material group | $Rz < 1\ \mu$m | $1 \le Rz \le 40\ \mu$m |
|:--|:--|:--|
| V, GGG (perl., bai.), Eh, IF (root), GTS (perl.) | 1.12 (84) | $1.674 - 0.529\,(Rz + 1)^{0.1}$ (87) |
| St | 1.07 (85) | $5.306 - 4.203\,(Rz + 1)^{0.01}$ (88) |
| GG, GGG (ferr.), NT, NV | 1.025 (86) | $4.299 - 3.259\,(Rz + 1)^{0.005}$ (89) |

For the static stress $Y_{R\,rel\,T} = 1.0$, formula (90).

**Size factor $Y_X$** (clause 15, table 5). Larger teeth have a larger volume of material under high
stress, so the permissible stress falls with the module. For $3 \times 10^6$ cycles:

| Material | $m_n \le 5$ | $5 < m_n <$ limit | $m_n \ge$ limit |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| St, V, GGG (perl., bai.), GTS (perl.) | 1.0 | $1.03 - 0.006\,m_n$ ($m_n < 30$) | 0.85 |
| Eh, IF (root), NT, NV | 1.0 | $1.05 - 0.01\,m_n$ ($m_n < 25$) | 0.8 |
| GG, GGG (ferr.) | 1.0 | $1.075 - 0.015\,m_n$ ($m_n < 25$) | 0.7 |

For the static stress $Y_X = 1.0$.

### Static strength

For a load that happens a few times in the life of the gear (the standard draws the line at stresses
permissible for $10^3$ cycles), $\sigma_{FP\,stat}$ is formula (5) with the static value of $Y_{NT}$
(table 3: 2.5 for St, V, GGG (perl., bai.), GTS, Eh and IF; 1.6 for GG, GGG (ferr.), NT and NV
(nitr.); 1.1 for NV (nitrocar.)), $Y_{\delta\,rel\,T}$ from (78)-(83), $Y_{R\,rel\,T} = 1.0$ and
$Y_X = 1.0$.

### In python-gearbox

$\sigma_{F\lim}$ is `Material.bending_stress_limit`, $S_{F\min}$ is
`Transmission.min_safety_factor_bending`, $Y_{ST}$ is fixed at 2.0 and the number of load cycles
is $N_L = $ `design_life_hours` $\cdot\,60\cdot$ rpm of each gear (the speed of the gear, not
of the pinion). The permissible stress and the safety factor are stored as `permissible_*` and
`safety_factor_*`; `permissible_*` already includes $S_{F\min}$ as in formula (5), and
$S_F = \sigma_{FP}\,S_{F\min}/\sigma_F$.

* $Y_{NT}$ interpolates table 3 on a log-log scale between its values at the end of the static
  range and at $3 \times 10^6$, and between $3 \times 10^6$ and $10^{10}$ it takes the **lower** line
  (0.85 at $10^{10}$ cycles). The curve is the one of the figure above.
* $Y_{R\,rel\,T}$ uses the Rz of the fillet, `Gear.root_surface_roughness` (by default the flank
  `surface_roughness`).
* $Y_X$ is piecewise linear in the module as in table 5, with the material group taken from
  `MaterialType`.
* $Y_{\delta\,rel\,T}$ follows (75)-(77) with the slip layer thickness of table 4.

`calculate_bending(transmission, static=True)` gives the static permissible stress: $Y_{NT}$ is the
static value, $Y_{R\,rel\,T} = Y_X = 1$, the design life is ignored, and $Y_{\delta\,rel\,T}$ comes
from (78)-(83). For normalized steel (`NORMALIZED_STEEL`, St, formula 78) and for through-hardened
steel or pearlitic ductile iron (formula 79) it needs `Material.yield_strength` ($\sigma_S$,
respectively $\sigma_{0.2}$), and raises `ValueError` without it.

## In python-gearbox

The whole calculation is one call:

```python
from gearbox import Gear, Lubricant, Material, MaterialType, ShaftArrangement, Tool, Transmission
from gearbox.standards import iso6336
from gearbox.standards.iso6336 import bending

tool = Tool(addendum_coefficient=1.0, dedendum_coefficient=1.25, root_fillet_radius_coefficient=0.38)
steel = Material(
    material_type=MaterialType.NITROCARBURIZED_STEEL,
    contact_stress_limit=1500.0, bending_stress_limit=460.0, brinell_hardness=286.7,
)
pinion = Gear(
    tool=tool, material=steel, number_of_teeth=22, module=2.5, face_width=34.0,
    quality_grade=6, surface_roughness=3.67, shaft_diameter=35.0,
    shaft_arrangement=ShaftArrangement.BETWEEN_BEARINGS_TOWARDS_TORQUED_END,
    bearing_span=60.0, offset=15.0, favorable_contact=True,
)
wheel = Gear(
    tool=tool, material=steel, number_of_teeth=40, module=2.5, face_width=34.0,
    quality_grade=6, surface_roughness=3.67, shaft_diameter=50.0,
    shaft_arrangement=ShaftArrangement.BETWEEN_BEARINGS_TOWARDS_TORQUED_END,
    bearing_span=60.0, offset=35.0, favorable_contact=True,
)
transmission = Transmission(
    pinion=pinion, wheel=wheel, lubricant=Lubricant(kinematic_viscosity_40c=160),
    pinion_speed_rpm=1450.0, power_kw=40.0, design_life_hours=10_000.0, application_factor=1.3,
)

result = iso6336.calculate_bending(transmission)
print(f"Y_F = {result.form_factor_pinion:.3f}   Y_S = {result.stress_correction_factor_pinion:.3f}")
print(f"sigma_F = {result.tooth_root_stress_pinion:.0f} MPa   "
      f"sigma_FP = {result.permissible_tooth_root_stress_pinion:.0f} MPa")
print(f"S_F = {result.safety_factor_pinion:.2f} (pinion)   {result.safety_factor_wheel:.2f} (wheel)")

root = bending._tooth_root_geometry(transmission, pinion)   # critical section of the pinion
print(f"s_Fn = {root.tooth_root_chord:.3f} mm   rho_F = {root.root_fillet_radius:.3f} mm")
print(f"h_Fe = {root.bending_lever_arm:.3f} mm   "
      f"alpha_Fen = {degrees(root.load_angle_at_load_point):.1f} deg")

static = iso6336.calculate_bending(transmission, static=True)
print(f"static: sigma_FP = {static.permissible_tooth_root_stress_pinion:.0f} MPa, "
      f"S_F = {static.safety_factor_pinion:.2f}")
```

sigma_F = 457 MPa   sigma_FP = 804 MPa
S_F = 1.76 (pinion)   1.88 (wheel)
s_Fn = 4.942 mm   rho_F = 1.419 mm
h_Fe = 2.466 mm   alpha_Fen = 18.3 deg
static: sigma_FP = 981 MPa, S_F = 2.15

`_tooth_root_geometry` starts with an underscore because it is an internal helper; the
{py:class}`~gearbox.standards.iso6336.bending.ToothRootGeometry` it returns is the documented

of the same pair is one `dataclasses.replace` away:

```python

shaped = replace(
    transmission, pinion=replace(pinion, tool=shaper), wheel=replace(wheel, tool=shaper)
)
print(iso6336.calculate_bending(shaped).form_factor_pinion)   # 1.439 instead of 1.530
```

### What the result contains

{py:class}`~gearbox.standards.iso6336.bending.BendingResult` has, for each gear (`_pinion` and
`_wheel` suffixes) unless a field is shared:

| Field | Symbol |
|:--|:--|
| `tooth_root_stress_*` | $\sigma_{F1}$, $\sigma_{F2}$ (MPa) |
| `permissible_tooth_root_stress_*` | $\sigma_{FP1}$, $\sigma_{FP2}$ (MPa, includes $S_{F\min}$) |
| `safety_factor_*` | $S_{F1}$, $S_{F2}$ |
| `form_factor_*`, `stress_correction_factor_*` | $Y_F$, $Y_S$ (or $Y_{Sg}$) |
| `helix_angle_factor`, `deep_tooth_factor` | $Y_\beta$, $Y_{DT}$ (the same for both gears) |
| `rim_thickness_factor_*` | $Y_B$ |
| `life_factor_*`, `relative_notch_sensitivity_*`, `relative_surface_factor_*`, `size_factor_*` | $Y_{NT}$, $Y_{\delta\,rel\,T}$, $Y_{R\,rel\,T}$, $Y_X$ |
| `stress_correction_factor_reference` | $Y_{ST} = 2$ |
| `dynamic_factor`, `transverse_load_factor`, `face_load_factor` | $K_v$, $K_{F\alpha}$, $K_{F\beta}$ |

{py:class}`~gearbox.standards.iso6336.bending.ToothRootGeometry` has `load_angle_at_load_point`
($\alpha_{Fen}$ of formula (25) in radians), `tooth_root_chord` ($s_{Fn}$), `root_fillet_radius`
($\rho_F$) and `bending_lever_arm` ($h_{Fe}$), the last three in mm.

## Limits and what is not covered

* **Scope of the standard.** The formulae apply to teeth conjugate to the basic rack of ISO 53 and,
  for other racks, if $\varepsilon_{\alpha n} < 2.5$; the user is cautioned to confirm results
  for $\beta > 30°$ and $\alpha_n > 25°$ by experience as by method A. The library does not check
  these ranges.
* **Method A and Annexes.** Annex A (permissible stress from notched or plain test pieces), Annex B
  (mean stress) and the reduced endurance of reversed loading, as in idler gears (it is "taken into
  consideration" in $\sigma_{F\lim}$, see ISO 6336-5), are not implemented: give a $\sigma_{F\lim}$ that
  already accounts for them.
* **Graphical values.** Figures 12 to 17 are replaced by the formulae they are consistent with.
  Formula (79) for a St steel *without* a well-defined yield point (steadily increasing elongation
  curve, $\sigma_{0.2}$) cannot be selected: `NORMALIZED_STEEL` always uses (78) with the yield
  point.
* **Slip layer thickness.** Table 4 gives $\rho'$ as a function of the tensile strength, yield stress
  or 0.2 % proof stress. The code interpolates the table with $\sigma_{F\lim}$ in their place.
  For the README pinion this changes $Y_{\delta\,rel\,T}$ by about 1 %.
* **Limited life.** The standard interpolates between $\sigma_{FP\,ref}$ and $\sigma_{FP\,stat}$ with
  formulae (6) to (8), so the static values of $Y_{\delta\,rel\,T}$, $Y_{R\,rel\,T}$ and $Y_X$ enter the
  curve. The code uses $Y_{NT}$ of table 3 with the factors of the reference stress, which agrees
  with (6)-(8) only if both states have the same factors. For the README pinion in case-hardened
  steel ($\sigma_{F\lim} = 430$ MPa) the code's $\sigma_{FP}$ is about 9 % above the formulae at
  $10^4$ cycles, 5 % at $10^5$ and 2 % at $10^6$; above $3 \times 10^6$ cycles there is no difference.
* **Tip rounding.** $d_a$ replaces $d_{Na}$ in formula (21); tip chamfers and rounding are ignored,
  as is the $q_s$ range of formula (62).
* **Tooth form of the figures and the finite-element model.** `ToothProfile` draws the rack-cut
  tooth (including the fillet of a hob, see {doc}`../geometry/index`); the protuberance $s_{pr}$ and
  the shaper cutter are not modelled there, though the strength calculation uses both.
* **Internal gears.** Shaper cutter only, and a rim thickness is required for $Y_B$; the
  interference checks between pinion and ring need ISO 21771 and are not available.
* **Double helical gears.** $b = 2 b_B$, $\varepsilon_\beta$ with $b_B$, and the load shared equally
  by both helices (see `KNOWN_ISSUES.md`).
